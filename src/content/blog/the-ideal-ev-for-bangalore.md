In 2024, I graduated and acquired a roughly 14 km commute from home to my office in HSR Layout, which is a perfectly reasonable distance until you remember that both ends of it are in Bangalore. I grew up here, and the traffic I remember from going to school in the 2010s feels almost pleasant compared to what the city has managed to achieve since. Getting to work was now a fairly substantial part of having a job.

My dad worked at Honda, so of course we had an Activa 125. Unfortunately, neither my riding style nor my legs were particularly happy with it. I'm about 6'3", and spending that much time on a scooter makes you very aware of where your knees are supposed to go. Petrol was also an increasingly annoying expense on an internship stipend. What do you mean ₹100 a litre, and I have to take a detour to a bunk to give you that money?

I had ridden a friend's first-generation Ather back in college and really liked it. I also tried the Ola S1 Pro, which I despise to this day. Sorry, Rao. I eventually booked a 450S, but then the Rizta launched and I switched to that. Mine has the 2.9 kWh battery, and it has been a dream to live with.

The comfort was the first thing I appreciated, and it continues to be one of my favourite things about it. There's enough room for my legs, the seat is comfortable, and I can settle into a riding position that works for my height. That matters a lot when a short distance on the map can turn into a long time on the road. The acceleration also feels great in Bangalore's constant starting and stopping, and the scooter feels stable underneath me, which makes the whole commute much less tiring.

The boot has probably changed my daily routine more than anything else. I don't wear a bag to the office anymore; my laptops go into this funky bag I picked up on sale at DailyObjects, which goes under the seat along with gym clothes, a raincoat and a shopping bag. Specifically, the Kalyan Silks bag that every Mallu household seems to have, because apparently those are issued to us at birth. Even with all of that, there's still room for the scooter's charger.

I also have a phone charger in the boot that can charge my MacBook Air, slowly. I don't think I would have put “can charge my laptop” on a scooter shopping checklist, but discovering that it could do it made me unreasonably happy.

Rain is a fairly important part of this story, given Bangalore's enthusiasm for turning underpasses and ORR service roads into water bodies. The Rizta's water resistance gives me some reassurance, and [this water-test video](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/bDom1AgWoi4) is worth a look. Ather's [published wading limit is 40 cm](https://cdn.bikedekho.com/upload/E-brochure/Ather%20Rizta/2492-Digital%20Rizta%20Brochure_30_June_25_compressed.pdf). I recommend leaving the pothole choreography to professionals like me, who have memorised the craters on their daily routes and literally get up from the seat to save their derrière from the torture. This is a very generous use of the word professional, and memorising potholes still doesn't tell me what's under floodwater, especially where an open drain or a change in depth could be hidden. There are also people forced onto the road because the footpaths somehow become even more dangerous when it rains. Krishna Byre Gowda avare, please get this fixed faster.

I didn't buy the Pro Pack. It was about ₹14,000 when I was buying the scooter, and I was paying for this out of my internship stipend, so that decision did not require a very elaborate spreadsheet. I have no regrets about skipping it. For my use, the scooter already does what I need, and I've become quite fond of having a useful everyday tool that I can get on and ride without feeling that I need to explore another set of features. I'm sure the extras are useful to some people; I haven't found myself missing them.

Ather's charging network has also worked really well for the places I go in Bangalore. Runs at Cubbon Park, visits to college on the Nagarbhavi side, and the occasional outstation expedition to Bellandur or Marathahalli have all been fairly stress-free. I know those last two are technically still in Bangalore, but I think anyone who has travelled there will understand.

If I need to stop at a charger for another twenty minutes, that's generally fine with me. I've roughly timed a 20% to 80% charge at around that on my 2.9 kWh scooter, although I haven't kept detailed charging logs, so treat that as my own rough observation. Having places to top up along the way has made a much bigger difference to my comfort with longer rides than I expected.

There are a few things that annoy me. The stand feels a little awkward, and I've had it slip on slopes, so I pay attention to where and how I park. Water also collects in the bolt holes. Some ornamental plastic at the front rattles, which I've quietened with a small piece of tape wedged into the gap. These are irritating things to discover on something I otherwise like this much.

I might also have convinced a colleague to buy the top-spec Rizta, and he doesn't seem particularly happy with the service quality, which is an awkward development when you've been enthusiastically recommending the thing. I hope Ather takes that seriously. Reading reports like [this one about a consumer commission ordering Ola to refund a faulty scooter and pay compensation](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/legal/news/faulty-ola-scooter-no-proper-service-consumer-panel-orders-rs-1-04-lakh-refund-compensation/articleshow/133146875.cms), I do feel Bhavish Aggarwal and Ola have set the bar so low that getting your scooter back in working order sounds like an achievement nowadays. I'd quite like Ather to aim a little higher than that.

My own running costs have been low: I'd put my total spend on servicing and public charging so far at under ₹2,000. That's an estimate for those two things, with home electricity separate. Ather's [current service guidance is every 5,000 km](https://www.atherenergy.com/blog/electric-scooter-service-guide), and servicing hasn't felt like a frequent interruption to owning it.

I think this is what I want more EVs built for our cities to get right: enough space for actual people and their things, a comfortable ride through miserable traffic, manageable running costs, and charging that fits into the places they already go. My enthusiasm for the Rizta mostly comes from using it every day and finding that it fits my life really well, right down to the gym clothes and the inevitable Kalyan Silks bag.

While I think Ultraviolette is absolutely amazing and representing Indian EVs well on the global stage, I don't see myself choosing it as a daily commuter, especially in Bangalore. For the kind of riding I do every day, I'd still want the comfort and practicality of a scooter. Where would I put the milk packets I get from the Nandini Booth ? Hang it off the handlebar ?

If you're considering getting an EV for your typical Bangalore city use, I'd go for the base Rizta, or, if you're happy to wait, try the [Konarc](https://www.atherenergy.com/konarc) before deciding. The TVS iQube and Vida V2 are also great choices in my books, and I'd actually put the TVS first for ride quality. My hesitation with the iQube comes from how the charging options would fit my routes and its [hub-mounted motor](https://www.tvsmotor.com/electric-scooters/tvs-iqube-price-in-india), which I'm personally less keen on. Ather's charging network has worked well enough for me that it carries a lot of weight in that decision.


The Rizta hasn't made HSR any closer to my house, but I am considerably happier about the journey now.

(I am not sponsored by Ather or Hero, I just like my working scooter and don't like Bhavish Aggarwal that much.)
