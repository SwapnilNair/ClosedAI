## Question

What changes when AI agents stop being isolated demonstrations and begin acting inside production environments with identities, tools, budgets, and access to business data?

## Working thesis

The largest risks will not come from a single dramatic model failure. They will emerge from ordinary systems problems amplified by autonomy: unclear ownership, excessive permissions, unpredictable token consumption, weak attribution, silent behavioral drift, and incident-response workflows that cannot reconstruct why an agent acted.

## Method

The work combines threat modeling, product experiments, conversations with security practitioners, and observation of how teams are already deploying AI workloads. The goal is to separate technically interesting attacks from problems enterprises can detect, prioritize, and act on.

## Current direction

I am particularly interested in blast-radius analysis, behavioral baselines, token-burn anomalies, and the evidence required to distinguish a compromised agent from a merely unreliable one.
